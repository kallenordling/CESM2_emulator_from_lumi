\section*{Definitions}

Let $s\in\{c,m\}$ index the side --- $c$ the CESM2 reference, $m$ the
emulator --- and let the experiment appear directly as a subscript,
$X\in\{\Xssp,\Xghg,\Xaer\}$:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
\item $\Xssp$ --- \textbf{all-forcing}. Every agent evolves together
  (greenhouse gases, aerosols, ozone, land use, biomass burning, solar,
  volcanic). Historical to 2014, SSP3-7.0 after; the window below sits
  entirely on the SSP3-7.0 side.
\item $\Xghg$ --- \textbf{GHG-only}. A single-forcing run in which only
  greenhouse gases vary; everything else is held at its 1850 value.
\item $\Xaer$ --- \textbf{aerosol-only}. Only anthropogenic aerosols
  vary, everything else at 1850.
\end{itemize}

The two single-forcing ensembles follow SSP3-7.0 after 2015 as well, so
all three experiments share one scenario pathway --- which is what makes
their difference meaningful --- and all three end at 2050, which is where
the single-forcing archive stops.

The averaging window is $\mathcal{W}=[2041,2050]$: after 2015, so no
major volcanic eruption falls inside it, and before 2050.

The evaluation files store per-member anomaly maps
$x^{s}_{X,i}(t,\phi,\lambda)$, each referenced to its own side's
$1850$--$1900$ climatology, for member $i=1,\dots,N^{s}_{X}$.

A bar over a symbol always means a mean over time, angle brackets a mean
over members, and double angle brackets a mean over Arctic area. The
$\Delta$ terms are emulator errors; $R$ is not an error but a property of
the climate response, present in CESM2 itself.

\section*{The calculation}

\paragraph{Step 1 --- decadal mean per member.}
\begin{equation}
\bar{x}^{s}_{X,i}(\phi,\lambda)
  \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{W}|}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{W}}
        x^{s}_{X,i}(t,\phi,\lambda)
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 2 --- ensemble mean.}
\begin{equation}
\bigl\langle x^{s}_{X}\bigr\rangle
  \;=\; \frac{1}{N^{s}_{X}}\sum_{i=1}^{N^{s}_{X}} \bar{x}^{s}_{X,i}
\end{equation}
Under equal sampling (\texttt{-{}-match-members}) the emulator ensemble is
truncated to the reference count, $N^{m}_{X}=N^{c}_{X}$, so that neither
side's mean is better converged than the other's. Here
$N_{\Xssp}=3$, $N_{\Xghg}=10$, $N_{\Xaer}=10$.

\paragraph{Step 3 --- the residual field.}
\begin{equation}
R^{s} \;=\; \bigl\langle x^{s}_{\Xssp}\bigr\rangle
          - \bigl\langle x^{s}_{\Xghg}\bigr\rangle
          - \bigl\langle x^{s}_{\Xaer}\bigr\rangle
\label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
$R^{s}$ is what the two agents fail to explain between them. It is not a
pure interaction term: the single-forcing runs hold ozone, land use,
biomass burning, solar and volcanic forcing at 1850, so
$R=(\text{other forcings})+(\text{nonlinear interaction})$.

\paragraph{Step 4 --- the Arctic operator.}
With area weights $w(\phi)=\cos\phi$, renormalised inside the mask
$\phi\geq 60^{\circ}\mathrm{N}$,
\begin{equation}
\ameanN{f}
  \;=\;
  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\phi\geq 60^{\circ}}\sum_{\lambda}
        w(\phi)\, f(\phi,\lambda)}
       {\displaystyle\sum_{\phi\geq 60^{\circ}}\sum_{\lambda} w(\phi)}
\end{equation}

\section*{The five bars}

The two end bars are the Arctic-mean residual on each side,
\begin{equation}
b_{1}=\ameanN{R^{c}}=+1.2180\ \mathrm{K},
\qquad
b_{5}=\ameanN{R^{m}}=+2.1416\ \mathrm{K}.
\end{equation}

The three middle bars are the emulator's bias for one experiment,
carrying the sign with which that experiment enters
Eq.~\eqref{eq:R}:
\begin{align}
\Delta_{\Xssp} &= +\ameanN{\langle x^{m}_{\Xssp}\rangle
   -\langle x^{c}_{\Xssp}\rangle}
   = +(4.674-4.242) = +0.4328\ \mathrm{K},\\
\Delta_{\Xghg} &= -\ameanN{\langle x^{m}_{\Xghg}\rangle
   -\langle x^{c}_{\Xghg}\rangle}
   = -(4.021-4.257) = +0.2354\ \mathrm{K},\\
\Delta_{\Xaer} &= -\ameanN{\langle x^{m}_{\Xaer}\rangle
   -\langle x^{c}_{\Xaer}\rangle}
   = -(-1.488+1.233) = +0.2554\ \mathrm{K}.
\end{align}

The sign flip is the substance of the panel: the emulator's $\Xghg$ run
is too \emph{cool} and its $\Xaer$ run too \emph{cold}, yet both terms
are \emph{subtracted} in Eq.~\eqref{eq:R}, so both push the residual
\emph{up}. Three biases of different physical sign act on $R$ in the same
direction.

\section*{Why the waterfall closes exactly}

\begin{equation}
\ameanN{R^{m}}-\ameanN{R^{c}}
 \;=\; \Delta_{\Xssp}+\Delta_{\Xghg}+\Delta_{\Xaer}
\label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation*}
1.2180+0.4328+0.2354+0.2554 \;=\; 2.1416
\end{equation*}

Equation~\eqref{eq:identity} is an identity, not a fit. Every operator in
the chain --- the decadal mean, the ensemble mean, the combination in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:R}, and the area-weighted mean --- is linear, so they
commute and
\begin{equation}
\ameanN{R^{m}}-\ameanN{R^{c}}
 =\ameanN{\;\bigl(\langle x^{m}_{\Xssp}\rangle\!-\!\langle x^{c}_{\Xssp}\rangle\bigr)
          -\bigl(\langle x^{m}_{\Xghg}\rangle\!-\!\langle x^{c}_{\Xghg}\rangle\bigr)
          -\bigl(\langle x^{m}_{\Xaer}\rangle\!-\!\langle x^{c}_{\Xaer}\rangle\bigr)}
\end{equation}
Verified numerically, the closure error is $1.2\times10^{-10}$~K, i.e.\
floating point. The panel therefore does not test anything: it
\emph{attributes} an already-known $0.924$~K gap to its three sources,
and the finding is that the split is roughly even rather than one bad
scenario.

\paragraph{Caveat.} After matching, $\Delta_{\Xssp}$ rests on three
members per side, all the CESM2 $\Xssp$ reference in these files
provides --- and that reference is the 3-member CMIP6 ensemble, whereas
$\Xghg$ and $\Xaer$ are 10-member LENS2 single-forcing runs. Part of
$R^{c}$ could therefore be a model-configuration difference rather than a
forcing interaction. It is both the largest bar and the least certain,
and it is the one that would move were the reference rewired to the ten
held-out LENS2 $\Xssp$ members. Bar labels in the figure are rounded to
two decimals, so reading them off gives $2.15$ rather than $2.14$; the
underlying values close exactly.
